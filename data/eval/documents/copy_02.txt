Locality-sensitive hashing is an algorithmic technique designed to solve the approximate nearest neighbor problem in high-dimensional vector spaces. Standard hashing algorithms seek to minimize collisions by distributing inputs uniformly across bucket keys. In contrast, locality-sensitive hashing intentionally maximizes collision probabilities for items that are close according to a specified distance metric. In the context of set similarity, MinHash permutations provide an unbiased estimator of the Jaccard similarity between two arbitrary token collections. By dividing the resulting MinHash signature vectors into several bands and hashing each band into discrete tables, candidate pairs can be screened in sub-quadratic computational complexity. Pairs that collide in at least one band are shortlisted for rigorous pairwise evaluation, making large-scale text similarity search feasible on modest hardware.
