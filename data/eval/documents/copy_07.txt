The quantum measurement problem concerns the transition between reversible linear unitary evolution and irreversible classical observation outcomes. Decoherence theory explains this phenomenon through continuous environmental entanglement, where microscopic quantum systems inevitably interact with ambient photons, phonons, and thermal fluctuations. These pervasive interactions disperse phase coherence into inaccessible macroscopic environmental degrees of freedom. Consequently, the reduced density matrix of the quantum system undergoes rapid diagonalization in a privileged pointer basis dictated by the interaction Hamiltonian. While environmental decoherence successfully accounts for the dynamic suppression of macroscopic quantum superpositions, it preserves global unitarity, leaving the philosophical selection of individual observed eigenvalues open to competing interpretations.
