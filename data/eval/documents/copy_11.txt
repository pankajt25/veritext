Traditional recurrent networks processed input tokens sequentially, creating an informational bottleneck for distant context dependencies. The multi-head self-attention mechanism addresses this limitation by calculating pairwise interaction scores between all positions in a sequence concurrently. Each attention head projects query, key, and value vectors into distinct representational subspaces, allowing the model to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at different positions. Positional encodings are subsequently added to the input representations to preserve the sequential order of elements. This structural formulation enables extensive parallelization during model training, dramatically decreasing computational duration while simultaneously improving contextual representations across diverse natural language understanding and machine translation benchmarks.
