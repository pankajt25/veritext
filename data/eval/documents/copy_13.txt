Cоnsеnsus рrоtосоls in distributеd systеms guаrаntее thаt а cluster of independent nodes agrees upon a unified replicated state machine despite network partitions and arbitrary node failures. Classical algorithms such as Paxos guarantee safety under asynchronous network conditions, though their complex operational mechanics present significant implementation challenges. The Raft consensus algorithm was formulated specifically to enhance understandability by decomposing consensus into independent sub-problems: leader election, log replication, and safety enforcement. Nodes transition dynamically between follower, candidate, and leader states based on deterministic heartbeat timers. A leader accepted by a majority quorum manages state transitions and commits log entries sequentially to stable disk storage. This architectural paradigm ensures that committed operations remain permanent and monotonically increasing across all operational replicas.
