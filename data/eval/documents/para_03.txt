Consensus mechanisms in decentralized environments ensure that clustered nodes maintain an identical replicated state, even when facing node outages or communication disruptions. Although classical protocols like Paxos ensure safety during asynchronous network conditions, their intricate operational principles often make practical implementation challenging. In contrast, the Raft protocol was structured specifically for conceptual clarity by partitioning the overall problem into distinct components: electing a leader, propagating state logs, and guaranteeing consistency. Server nodes alternate between follower, candidate, and leader roles governed by timed heartbeats. The elected quorum leader coordinates state modifications and records entries permanently onto disk, guaranteeing that confirmed transactions persist immutably throughout the operational network.
