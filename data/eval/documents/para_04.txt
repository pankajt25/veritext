Photosynthetic assimilation represents the biological sequence by which autotrophs convert ambient carbon dioxide into nutritious sugars. The dark Calvin-Benson reaction starts when the RuBisCO enzyme adds carbon to a five-carbon acceptor molecule. The resulting unstable compound splits into twin molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate, which are subsequently reduced using ATP and NADPH generated during sunlight absorption. These triose molecules are then harvested for carbohydrate production or used to regenerate the initial sugar acceptor. In hotter environments, competing oxygenation reactions cause photorespiratory energy losses, prompting evolutionary adaptations such as C4 and CAM pathways that isolate carbon concentration inside specialized plant compartments.
