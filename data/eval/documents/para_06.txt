Subduction boundaries are geological collision zones where heavy oceanic plates sink beneath lighter continental masses into the mantle. As this water-rich oceanic crust descends into regions of increasing heat and pressure, dehydration metamorphic changes release trapped moisture into the surrounding mantle wedge. This fluid influx dramatically lowers the melting point of neighboring peridotite rocks, producing buoyant basaltic and andesitic magma. The rising molten material fuels active volcanic chains and aids in the formation of continental landmasses. Meanwhile, locked fault surfaces along the plate contact store massive tectonic tension, occasionally unleashing severe megathrust earthquakes capable of triggering devastating oceanwide tsunamis.
