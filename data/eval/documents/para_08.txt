Epigenetic mechanisms encompass transmissible variations in cellular expression and phenotypic traits that occur without altering the underlying DNA sequence. Structural chromatin remodeling, including DNA methylation and chemical histone modifications, determines whether specific genomic regions remain accessible to RNA polymerase. Adding methyl groups to promoter CpG islands commonly suppresses transcription by recruiting binding proteins that condense chromatin into dense heterochromatin. Conversely, histone acetyltransferases attach acetyl groups to lysine residues, reducing positive charges and loosening DNA-histone bonds. These dynamic molecular modifications maintain stable cell identity while remaining adaptable to metabolic cues.
