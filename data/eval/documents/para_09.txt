Standard economic models assume that human actors behave as rational utility maximizers possessing unlimited reasoning abilities and fixed goals. Behavioral economics refutes this assumption by highlighting consistent cognitive shortcuts that depart from rational rules. Prospect theory indicates that people assess outcomes as profits or losses measured against a subjective baseline rather than overall wealth. Furthermore, empirical curves demonstrate an S-shape with distinct loss aversion, meaning the emotional pain of losing exceeds the satisfaction of gaining an equal amount. Other bounded rationality traits, including present-bias discounting and contextual framing, significantly impact market prices and household savings behavior.
