Bacterial biofilms are dense colonies of microorganisms embedded in a protective matrix of extracellular polymers, including proteins, sugars, and free DNA. This protective architecture establishes steep gradients in nutrient availability, pH, and oxygen, nurturing specialized dormant persister cells. In medical environments, biofilms adhere strongly to artificial implants and biological membranes, exhibiting resistance against antibiotics that can be hundreds of times higher than that of free-swimming cells. Physical diffusion limitations hinder drug entry, while matrix enzymes and elevated gene transfer accelerate the spread of multi-drug resistance across the bacterial population.
