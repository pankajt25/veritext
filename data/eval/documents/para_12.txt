The urban heat island effect highlights how cities consistently register warmer temperatures than adjacent undeveloped rural zones. Human heat generation, diminished plant evapotranspiration, and ubiquitous dark construction materials like concrete absorb immense solar radiation during the day. During nighttime hours, this stored thermal mass releases energy sluggishly, impairing human rest and driving up air conditioning electricity consumption. Effective remedies center on expanding urban vegetation, including planted roofs, extensive tree planting, and permeable ground surfaces. High-reflectance coatings reflect sunlight back into the atmosphere, decreasing peak surface heat.
