Synaptic malleability serves as the cellular foundation for learning and memory storage across the nervous system. Long-term potentiation, first documented in hippocampal tissue, involves a sustained increase in communication strength across synapses following repetitive electrical stimulation. This enhancement requires postsynaptic depolarization strong enough to displace magnesium ions that block NMDA receptor channels. Inflowing calcium ions then trigger CaMKII enzymes, which modify existing AMPA receptors and drive the insertion of new receptors into the postsynaptic membrane, strengthening subsequent excitatory responses.
