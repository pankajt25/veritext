Cellular automata represent mathematical dynamical models composed of a grid of discrete cells that switch between finite allowable states. Updates happen synchronously at regular intervals following uniform local rules that consider solely the states of adjacent neighboring cells. Despite their straightforward design, deterministic cellular automata frequently display complex emergent patterns, organization, and computational completeness, as seen in Conway's Game of Life. Wolfram's taxonomy splits automata behaviors into four categories, from static uniformity to chaotic randomness and complex computational forms, demonstrating how rich behaviors emerge from simple foundations.
