Deep sea hydrothermal vents located along ocean ridges host vibrant chemosynthetic biological systems without requiring sunlight. Frigid ocean water seeps into fractured volcanic bedrock, where magma sources heat the liquid up to several hundred degrees Celsius while leaching minerals and sulfur compounds. Upon venting into the freezing ocean depths, these dissolved minerals precipitate quickly to form towering mineral chimneys known as black smokers. Chemosynthetic bacteria harness energy from oxidising hydrogen sulfide, forming the base of the food chain that sustains specialized vent creatures such as gigantic tube worms and vent clams.
