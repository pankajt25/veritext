Instead of stepping through text token by token—a habit that severely crippled earlier recurrent models whenever associations spanned long gaps—modern sequence learning depends predominantly on pairwise attention calculations performed across all positions at once. This broad architectural change permits training routines to scale across modern parallel graphics hardware, yielding rapid speedups without sacrificing contextual fidelity. When inputs enter the pipeline, they receive additive positional signals to retain sequential arrangement. Within the network, individual attention projections split the representations into isolated geometric spaces via separate query, key, and value transformations, allowing simultaneous focus on heterogeneous syntactic and semantic dependencies. Consequently, models achieve competitive empirical metrics on benchmark translation and natural language comprehension tasks with markedly lower training latency.
