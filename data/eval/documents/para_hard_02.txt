Evaluating all potential document pairs becomes computationally intractable for substantial text corpora, prompting practitioners to employ locality-sensitive hashing to isolate probable neighbors in high-dimensional representations. Rather than dispersing hash values evenly to avoid identical bucket placements as conventional cryptographic routines strive to do, locality-sensitive methods intentionally boost the chances of key collisions for nearby points under chosen metrics. In string comparison frameworks, MinHash signatures function as mathematically unbiased estimators for pairwise Jaccard overlap between shingle vocabularies. Partitioning these signature arrays into distinct row groups or bands ensures that any item sharing an identical band segment gets shortlisted into candidate buckets. Because only candidate pairs proceed to full pairwise comparison, total comparison time drops from quadratic levels down to manageable sub-quadratic regimes, enabling responsive analysis even when executed on modest workstation hardware.
