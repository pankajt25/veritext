In decentralized network architectures where individual nodes fail or communication lines suffer transient partitions, maintaining identical state machines across all participating servers requires formal agreement algorithms. Paxos established theoretical correctness under asynchronous delays, but engineering robust implementations proved notoriously difficult for systems practitioners. To mitigate this conceptual complexity, the Raft protocol structures the consensus lifecycle into modular components, isolating leader selection, entry replication, and state validation into self-contained operational routines. Participating machines cycle between follower, candidate, and coordinator roles governed by randomized interval timers. Once elected by a strict majority ballot, the coordinator synchronizes incoming requests sequentially and appends committed transitions to non-volatile disk logs, ensuring that state transitions proceed deterministically and irreversibly across the cluster.
