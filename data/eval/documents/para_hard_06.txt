At tectonic convergent margins, cold and water-saturated oceanic slabs sink beneath adjacent continental plates into the deeper mantle. As descending rocks encounter increasing lithostatic pressures and elevated geothermal gradients, metamorphic recrystallization expels structural water into the overlying peridotite mantle wedge. The influx of these aqueous fluids depresses the melting point of mantle rock, triggering flux melting that produces buoyant calc-alkaline magmas. Upon rising through the crust, these magmas supply volcanic island arcs and gradually foster continental crustal growth. Concurrently, high frictional coupling along the subduction interface traps substantial elastic deformation over centuries, which eventually releases abruptly during massive megathrust seismic dislocations that trigger destructive oceanwide tsunamis.
