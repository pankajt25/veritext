The conflict between deterministic unitary state trajectories and stochastic, non-reversible measurement outcomes remains a central dilemma in quantum mechanics. According to environmental decoherence, no microscopic state remains truly isolated, as interactions with ambient thermal fluctuations, scattered photons, and lattice vibrations continuously entangle the quantum system with macroscopic surroundings. This leaking of relative phase correlations quickly scatters coherent superpositions across unobservable external degrees of freedom. As a result, the subsystem's density matrix diagonalizes along specific pointer states determined by the governing interaction Hamiltonian. While decoherence successfully clarifies why macroscopic quantum superpositions seem to vanish instantaneously in laboratory observations, the global wavefunction remains unitary, leaving open fundamental interpretational questions regarding why specific outcomes become actualized.
