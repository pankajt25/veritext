Heritable modifications in gene expression patterns that occur without any alteration to nucleotide sequences fall under the domain of epigenetic regulation. Chemical chromatin remodeling processes, notably DNA cytosine methylation and enzymatic histone tail tagging, dictate whether transcription factors can physically contact specific genomic loci. For instance, when CpG-rich promoter stretches become methylated, they recruit specialized methyl-binding protein complexes that condense open euchromatin into tightly packed, transcriptionally inert heterochromatin. In contrast, histone acetyltransferases append acetyl groups to lysine residues, neutralizing positive charges and relaxing histone affinities for the negatively charged DNA backbone. These dynamic chromatin adaptations preserve distinct lineage commitments across cell divisions while still preserving regulatory flexibility when environmental cues change.
