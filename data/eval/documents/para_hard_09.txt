Standard neoclassical economic modeling presumes that economic actors behave as strictly rational decision agents endowed with stable preferences and flawless computational capabilities. Behavioral economists disputed this dogma by revealing repeatable heuristics and cognitive biases that depart fundamentally from optimal utility maximization. Under prospect theory, individuals assess alternative scenarios as perceived gains or losses measured against a subjective baseline rather than total integrated wealth positions. The characteristic value curve exhibits an asymmetrical S-profile, demonstrating diminishing sensitivity alongside pronounced loss aversion, whereby the psychological pain inflicted by a financial setback far outweighs the satisfaction obtained from an equivalent monetary windfall. When coupled with hyperbolic discounting and context-dependent choice framing, these behavioral traits drive significant distortions in market pricing and private savings allocations.
