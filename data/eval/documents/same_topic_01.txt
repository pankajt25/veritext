Sequence processing in contemporary neural networks is dominated by transformer architectures powered by scaled dot-product attention. Instead of passing hidden states step by step like recurrent networks, self-attention maps an input sequence to queries, keys, and values through learned linear transformations. The inner product between queries and keys determines attention weights after normalization through a softmax function, dictating how much weight each token assigns to context elements. Multi-head designs execute this calculation across parallel heads, allowing separate representation subspaces to capture grammatical patterns and long-range semantics simultaneously. While recurrent models struggle with vanishing gradients across extensive contexts, transformers maintain direct paths between all positions, albeit at quadratic computational complexity relative to sequence length.
