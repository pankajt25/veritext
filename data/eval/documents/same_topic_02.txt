Nearest-neighbor search across massive high-dimensional datasets often suffers from the curse of dimensionality, rendering exact distance searches computationally intractable. Locality-sensitive hashing mitigates this burden by mapping data points into discrete hash buckets such that similar vectors have a substantially higher probability of collision than dissimilar ones. For binary sets and bag-of-words representations, the MinHash scheme evaluates the minimum hash value under random permutations to estimate Jaccard similarity without computing full set intersections. Combining multiple hash functions into bands tunes the false-positive and false-negative trade-offs via an S-shaped probability curve. Candidate pairs retrieved from matching buckets undergo exact verification, dramatically shrinking the search space for large-scale document deduplication and nearest-neighbor clustering.
