Fault-tolerant distributed storage systems depend on consensus algorithms to ensure consistency across replicated state machines in the presence of node crashes and network delays. Raft provides a practical alternative to Paxos by explicitly separating consensus into distinct phases: leader election, log replication, and commitment. In Raft, cluster members operate as followers, candidates, or a single elected leader. Randomized election timers prevent split-vote scenarios when followers fail to receive periodic heartbeats from their leader. Once elected through majority approval, the leader accepts client commands, appends them to its local log, and broadcasts them to peer nodes. An entry is committed and safely applied to the state machine only after a quorum confirms its disk persistence, guaranteeing linearizable read and write semantics.
