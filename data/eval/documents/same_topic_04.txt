Photosynthesis powers terrestrial biomes by converting solar energy into chemical bonds through light-dependent reactions and the stroma-localized Calvin cycle. In the fixation phase, the carboxylase enzyme Rubisco attaches carbon dioxide to ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate, generating unstable six-carbon intermediates that rapidly split into two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate. Chemical reduction follows, utilizing ATP and NADPH generated during thylakoid electron transport to produce glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate molecules. Most triose phosphates are channeled through enzymatic rearrangements to regenerate the initial ribulose acceptor, while the remainder serves as raw material for starch and hexose synthesis. However, Rubisco also binds molecular oxygen, triggering wasteful photorespiration that reduces net photosynthetic efficiency under dry or hot ambient conditions.
