Convergent plate margins are geological zones where old, cold oceanic lithosphere sinks back into the asthenospheric mantle along deep oceanic trenches. The descending slab carries hydrated marine sediments and basaltic crust into high-pressure, high-temperature metamorphic environments, triggering dehydration reactions that expel aqueous fluids into the overlying peridotite mantle wedge. The addition of water depresses the rock melting point through flux melting, generating buoyant magmas that rise through the crust to form volcanic island arcs and continental volcanoes. The mechanical coupling between colliding tectonic plates along the subduction interface frequently becomes locked, accumulating significant elastic stress over centuries until catastrophic rupture produces megathrust earthquakes capable of displacing ocean basins and generating destructive tsunamis.
