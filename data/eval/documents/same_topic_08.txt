Epigenetic regulation controls gene expression profiles without altering the primary sequence of nucleotides encoded within genomic DNA. Eukaryotic chromatin structure toggles between transcriptionally active euchromatin and tightly packed, inaccessible heterochromatin through enzymatic post-translational modifications. DNA methyltransferases add methyl groups to cytosine residues within CpG islands, often recruiting repressive chromatin-remodeling complexes that silence promoter activity. In contrast, histone acetyltransferases append acetyl groups to lysine tails on histone core octamers, weakening electrostatic attraction to negatively charged DNA backbones and promoting transcription factor binding. These molecular modifications establish cellular lineages during embryonic development while remaining responsive to physiological cues, environmental stresses, and nutritional availability.
