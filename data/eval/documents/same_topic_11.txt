Graph neural networks process complex non-grid relational data, such as chemical molecules, knowledge graphs, and social interactions, by learning over irregular graph topologies. Modern message-passing frameworks update node representations through iterative rounds of local neighborhood feature aggregation. In each layer, a node collects embedding vectors from its connected neighbors, combines them using permutation-invariant pooling operations like sum or mean, and passes the result through nonlinear neural layers to update its internal state. While message passing effectively models multi-hop structural relationships, stacking many graph convolutional layers often leads to over-smoothing, wherein node embeddings across the entire connected component become indistinguishable, degrading downstream node classification and link prediction accuracy.
