Urban centers frequently register significantly warmer ambient and surface temperatures than adjacent rural countryside, an environmental condition referred to as the urban heat island effect. Replacement of natural vegetative land cover with impervious, low-albedo construction materials like asphalt and concrete causes cities to absorb and store massive quantities of solar radiation during daytime hours. Waste heat released by vehicle exhaust, industrial machinery, and domestic air conditioning systems exacerbates this thermal retention. At night, trapped heat radiates back into the urban boundary layer, impairing night cooling and increasing heat-related morbidity. Strategic mitigation measures involve retrofitting urban envelopes with high-reflectance cool roofs, expanding urban tree canopies, and installing extensive green roofs to promote cooling via evapotranspiration.
