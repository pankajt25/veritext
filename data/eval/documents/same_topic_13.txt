Learning and memory consolidation in the central nervous system depend upon activity-dependent synaptic plasticity, which dynamically modulates the strength of chemical communication between connected neurons. Long-term potentiation in the mammalian hippocampus provides the foremost cellular model for persistent synaptic enhancement following brief, high-frequency electrical stimulation. Induction occurs when repetitive glutamate release depolarizes the postsynaptic membrane sufficiently to relieve the voltage-dependent magnesium ion block within NMDA receptor channels. Calcium ions flood through the open pore, triggering intracellular signaling cascades governed by calcium-dependent protein kinases. These enzymes phosphorylate postsynaptic AMPA receptors and mobilize vesicular stores to insert additional AMPA channels into the synaptic cleft, increasing subsequent synaptic transmission amplitude.
