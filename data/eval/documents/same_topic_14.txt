Cellular automata are idealized mathematical models of discrete dynamical systems that demonstrate how intricate macroscopic complexity can arise spontaneously from simple microscopic rules. An automaton consists of a regular spatial lattice of cells, each holding one of a finite set of discrete states. At each synchronous discrete clock tick, every cell updates its status based entirely on a deterministic transition function applied to its local neighborhood. John Conway Game of Life famously demonstrated that minimal two-dimensional birth and survival rules can support self-replicating patterns and universal Turing computation. In his classification scheme, Stephen Wolfram categorized cellular automata into four fundamental behavioral regimes: uniform convergence, periodic cycling, chaotic dispersion, and complex localized structures capable of information processing.
