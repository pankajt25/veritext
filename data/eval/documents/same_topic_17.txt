Semiconductor nanowires are cylindrical nanoscale crystals with diameters constrained to tens of nanometers while maintaining axial lengths spanning multiple micrometers. When the radial dimensions of a nanowire approach the exciton Bohr radius of the constituent semiconductor material, carrier motion becomes restricted in two spatial dimensions, giving rise to quantum confinement effects. This physical confinement alters the continuous electronic density of states into quantized one-dimensional sub-bands, effectively expanding the material optical bandgap and enhancing radiative recombination efficiency. Grown primarily via metal-catalyzed vapor-liquid-solid synthesis, nanowires can accommodate significant lattice mismatches without creating dislocation defects. Consequently, they serve as building blocks for integrated nanosensors, nanoscale field-effect transistors, and sub-wavelength coherent laser sources.
