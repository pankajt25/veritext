Fair division is a branch of discrete mathematics and algorithmic game theory that studies methods for dividing indivisible or divisible goods among autonomous agents with diverse subjective valuations. In divisible cake-cutting scenarios, the primary fairness criterion is envy-freeness, which requires that no participant prefers another agent assigned portion over their own. For two participants, the classic cut-and-choose algorithm guarantees an envy-free and proportional outcome, where one agent cuts the cake into two equal-value pieces and the second chooses their preferred slice. When allocating among three or more agents, discrete protocols like the Selfridge-Conway and Brams-Taylor algorithms provide bounded or finite query solutions. For indivisible items, researchers focus on envy-freeness up to one item and maximizing Nash social welfare to balance equity and efficiency.
