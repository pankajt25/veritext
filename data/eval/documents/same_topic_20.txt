Cataclysmic super-eruptions ejecting thousands of cubic kilometers of pyroclastic rock and ash into the atmosphere exert profound multi-year impacts on Earth climate and biosphere. Highly energetic convective plumes propel tens of millions of tons of sulfur dioxide gas deep into the dry stratosphere, far above the tropospheric hydrological cycle that washes out aerosols. In the stratosphere, sulfur dioxide oxidizes in the presence of water vapor to form tiny reflective sulfate aerosols that encircle the globe within weeks. These persistent stratospheric aerosols backscatter incoming solar radiation, driving steep drops in global mean surface temperatures, altering precipitation patterns, and causing severe agricultural collapse. Major prehistoric events like the Toba super-eruption demonstrate how volcanic winters can plunge continents into rapid cooling and impose severe demographic bottlenecks.
