Photosynthetic carbon fixation is the biochemical pathway through which autotrophic organisms assimilate atmospheric carbon dioxide into organic carbohydrates. The light-independent Calvin-Benson cycle initiates when the enzyme ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase catalyzes the carboxylation of a five-carbon sugar substrate. This unstable intermediate cleaves into two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate, which undergo enzymatic reduction driven by adenosine triphosphate and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate produced during initial photochemical reactions. Triose phosphates are subsequently exported for sucrose synthesis or recycled to regenerate the initial ribulose acceptor. Under elevated temperatures, oxygenase activity competes directly with carboxylation, leading to photorespiratory energy losses that selective adaptations like C4 and CAM metabolism circumvent through specialized cellular compartmentalization.
