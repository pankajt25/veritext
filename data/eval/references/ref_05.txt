Zero-knowledge proofs permit a mathematical prover to convince a skeptical verifier that an asserted computational statement is true without divulging any auxiliary knowledge beyond the statement's validity. Formally defined through the properties of completeness, soundness, and zero-knowledge, these interactive protocols transform computational relations into verifiable arithmetic circuits. Non-interactive zero-knowledge arguments of knowledge utilize common reference strings and elliptic curve pairings to compress proofs into succinct cryptographic commitments that any participant can verify rapidly. Applications include privacy-preserving digital identity validation, confidential transactions on distributed ledgers, and verifiable off-chain computation, where resource-constrained devices delegate heavy algebraic workloads to untrusted servers.
