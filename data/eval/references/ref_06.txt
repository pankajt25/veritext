Subduction zones represent convergent boundaries where dense oceanic lithosphere descends beneath lighter continental or island-arc crust into the Earth's mantle. As the hydrated oceanic slab penetrates into regions of elevated temperature and lithostatic pressure, metamorphic dehydration reactions release mineral-bound water into the overlying mantle wedge. The introduction of volatile fluids significantly reduces the solidus temperature of peridotite rocks, inducing partial melting that generates basaltic and andesitic magmas. These ascending buoyant magmas feed active volcanic arcs and construct continental crust. Furthermore, locked interface boundaries along the subduction interface accumulate enormous elastic strain, periodically discharging seismic energy in catastrophic megathrust earthquake ruptures that generate oceanwide tsunami waves.
