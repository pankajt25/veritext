Epigenetics encompasses heritable alterations in cellular phenotype and transcriptional activity that do not involve modifications to the underlying deoxyribonucleic acid sequence. Covalent chromatin remodeling mechanisms, including DNA cytosine methylation and post-translational histone modifications, govern the structural accessibility of genomic loci to RNA polymerase complexes. Methylation of CpG islands within promoter regions typically promotes transcriptional silencing by recruiting methyl-CpG-binding domain proteins that orchestrate chromatin compaction into condensed heterochromatin. Conversely, histone acetyltransferase enzymes catalyze the addition of acetyl groups to lysine residues, neutralizing positive electrostatic charges and loosening histone-DNA interactions. These dynamic epigenetic marks establish stable cellular identity during differentiation while exhibiting reversible plasticity in response to metabolic signals.
