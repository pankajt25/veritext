Graph neural networks generalize deep learning architectures to non-Euclidean relational domains where data instances are represented as nodes and edges in irregular topologies. The fundamental operational mechanism relies upon message passing, wherein each node aggregates feature representations from its immediate topological neighborhood across successive computational layers. An update function subsequently combines the aggregated neighborhood embedding with the node's prior hidden state. Permutation equivariance guarantees that graph topological isomorphisms produce identical representational transformations. However, deep graph networks frequently encounter over-smoothing phenomena, where representations across distinct connected nodes converge toward uniform vectors, constraining effective architectural depth in chemical property prediction and social network analysis.
