The urban heat island phenomenon describes the documented microclimatic condition where metropolitan areas experience markedly higher ambient temperatures than surrounding rural hinterlands. Anthropogenic heat emissions, reduced vegetative evapotranspiration, and the widespread deployment of low-albedo construction materials like asphalt and concrete drive daytime thermal absorption. At nighttime, stored thermal energy radiates slowly, impairing human physiological recovery and escalating cooling energy demands. Sustainable mitigation strategies prioritize green infrastructure implementation, including intensive vegetated roofs, urban tree canopy expansion, and permeable urban pavements. High-albedo cool roofs and retroreflective building envelopes reflect incident solar radiation directly back into space, lowering peak urban surface temperatures by several degrees Celsius.
