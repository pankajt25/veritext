Direct air capture technologies extract carbon dioxide directly from ambient atmosphere to achieve net-negative greenhouse gas emissions. Because ambient carbon dioxide concentrations are extremely dilute, approximately four hundred and twenty parts per million, large volumes of air must be processed through specialized contactors. Chemical absorption systems utilize strong liquid alkaline hydroxide solutions, converting gaseous carbon dioxide into dissolved carbonates that subsequently undergo calcination at high temperatures to release pure carbon dioxide gas. Solid adsorption systems employ amine-functionalized porous membranes that capture molecules via chemisorption and release them during low-temperature vacuum desorption cycles. Geological sequestration subsequently injects the captured gas into deep basaltic formations for permanent mineral trapping.
