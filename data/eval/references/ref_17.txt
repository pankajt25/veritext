Semiconductor nanowires are quasi-one-dimensional anisotropic nanostructures characterized by diameters on the order of tens of nanometers and lengths spanning several micrometers. When physical wire diameters become comparable to the bulk exciton Bohr radius, spatial quantum confinement alters the electronic band structure, discretizing continuous energy bands into distinct sub-bands. This confinement effect widens the effective optical bandgap, permitting precise spectral tunability across optoelectronic devices. Bottom-up vapor-liquid-solid growth mechanisms facilitate the synthesis of defect-free crystalline structures with sharp axial and radial heterojunctions. These unique physical properties make semiconductor nanowires promising candidates for sub-wavelength coherent nanolasers, high-mobility field-effect transistors, and high-efficiency photovoltaic energy conversion devices.
