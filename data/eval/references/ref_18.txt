The mathematical formulation of linear perspective during the early Italian Renaissance fundamentally transformed European visual representation by projecting three-dimensional spatial coordinates onto a planar pictorial surface. Architect Filippo Brunelleschi demonstrated the geometric construction empirically through mirror experiments in Florence, while Leon Battista Alberti codified the theoretical principles in his treatise De pictura. The system establishes a central vanishing point situated on a horizontal eye-level horizon line, toward which all orthogonal visual rays converge proportionally. Transversals intersect these orthogonals at geometrically decreasing intervals, simulating retinal optical foreshortening and atmospheric depth. This scientific synthesis of Euclidean geometry and artistic technique mirrored the humanist cultural conviction that empirical human perception could organize and master the physical world.
