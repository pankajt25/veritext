Fair division algorithms address the mathematical challenge of partitioning a set of heterogeneous resources among multiple autonomous agents with divergent subjective utility functions. In cake-cutting problems involving divisible goods, the classical cut-and-choose protocol achieves envy-freeness for two participants, ensuring that neither agent prefers the portion allocated to the other. Scaling fair division to three or more agents requires complex moving-knife procedures or discrete query algorithms such as the Brams-Taylor procedure. For indivisible items, exact envy-free allocations may not exist, shifting theoretical focus toward envy-freeness up to one item and the maximization of Nash social welfare, which balances efficiency and fairness by maximizing the geometric mean of agent utilities.
