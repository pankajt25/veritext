Volcanic super-eruptions discharging hundreds of cubic kilometers of pyroclastic tephra into the atmosphere induce severe, multi-year perturbations in global climate dynamics. Highly buoyant convective eruption columns inject tens of teragrams of sulfur dioxide gas directly into the stratosphere, exceeding the altitude of tropospheric precipitation scavenging. Oxidation of sulfur dioxide produces sub-micron sulfate aerosols that persist for several years, scattering incoming solar radiation back into space. The resulting radiative forcing causes significant global surface cooling, disruption of monsoon circulation regimes, and widespread agricultural failure. Historic examples such as the Toba and Tambora eruptions illustrate how extreme volcanic aerosol loading can trigger abrupt volcanic winters that precipitate ecological collapse and severe human demographic stress.
