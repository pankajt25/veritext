# Semantic Integrity Verification in Academic Writing

## Abstract
Ensuring academic integrity requires multi-modal inspection strategies that encompass lexical overlap, semantic consistency, and stylistic variance. This study proposes an open architecture for modular text verification.

## Background
Educational institutions worldwide report increasing difficulties in distinguishing between original student prose and synthetic machine outputs. According to pedagogical research:

> Integrity in higher education is not merely punitive compliance; it constitutes the cornerstone of academic inquiry and authentic critical engagement.

When students engage with automated tools, boundary lines between inspiration and unauthorized synthesis become ambiguous.

## Experimental Framework
We collect essays across multiple disciplines and segment documents into paragraphs and sentences. Each sentence is mapped with exact character offsets to support fine-grained report generation and UI highlighting.

## Conclusion
A transparent verification framework provides interpretable similarity metrics rather than opaque binary classifications.
